About Malawi

A country in south-east Africa, known for the warmth of its people and at the same time one of the poorest in the world.

Land & people

The "Warm Heart of Africa".

Malawi is a landlocked country in south-east Africa, bordering Tanzania, Zambia and Mozambique. A large part of it belongs to Lake Malawi, which stretches across almost a fifth of the country. For the friendliness of its people it is often called "The Warm Heart of Africa". Economically, Malawi is one of the poorest countries in the world.

The emergency department this campaign is about is located in Blantyre, in the south of the country.

History & people

A young country with an eventful history.

Malawi gained independence from Britain in 1964. Its first three decades were shaped by Hastings Kamuzu Banda, who ruled the country as a one-party state until 1994. Since 1994 Malawi has been a multi-party democracy.

Since October 2025 Peter Mutharika has been president. He won the September 2025 election against the incumbent Lazarus Chakwera; his vice-president is Jane Ansah. The campaign was dominated by the strained economy – high prices, a weak currency and scarce foreign exchange shape everyday life for many.

Malawi is a very young country: a large share of the roughly 20.7 million people are under 18, and most live in rural areas and from agriculture. There are many ethnic groups – among them Chewa, Lomwe, Yao, Ngoni and Tumbuka – who mostly live together peacefully. The official languages are Chichewa and English.

Culture

Community, faith and nsima.

What struck me immediately in Malawi is the strong sense of community. Family and neighbours carry a lot: people share what they have and look out for one another – especially where state support is missing. It's not for nothing that Malawi is called the "Warm Heart of Africa".

Faith plays a big role. Most of the population is Christian, and in the south and along the lake there are also many Muslims. Churches and congregations are important places of togetherness – services with lots of singing are a fixed part of the week for many.

On the plate there is usually nsima: a firm porridge made from maize flour, eaten with almost every meal – together with vegetables, beans, sometimes fish from Lake Malawi (such as chambo) or meat. Traditionally it is eaten by hand, often shared from one bowl.

Poverty & health

Why poverty here is also a health question.

Around 70 % of people live on less than 2.15 US dollars a day. Clean water, sanitation and medical care are not a given in many places, especially in rural areas.

On top of that comes a high burden of disease: malaria, HIV and tuberculosis are widespread, and many children and mothers die from things that would be easily treatable elsewhere.
